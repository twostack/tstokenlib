Pool Round Anatomy

Every transaction in two consecutive rounds, which script locks which output, and which later input makes it run. Round 1 takes in a deposit and round 2 pays it out. Sizes are measured from two chains mined on a regtest node, one at test parameters and one at production parameters, with the real verifier and real root proofs.

Reading this

A script is named for the output it locks

PP3 is the locking script on round N's output 3. It executes when round N+1 spends it at input 3. Those are two different positions with two different numbers, and the page keeps them apart everywhere: a box's outputs are what it creates, and an arrow arriving at a box is what consumes them.

Three relationships appear in the map, and telling them apart is the whole design. A solid arrow is a spend. A dotted line is a claim made about a transaction that has not been spent yet: PP3 pins an outpoint the next round must spend, and PP1 certifies what the script at that outpoint actually is. A grey arrow from a slot transaction down to a round is the anchor: the round that pins Y_N also spends Y_N's output 1, so the transaction it pins is already mined.

The chain

Rounds and witnesses alternate; slot transactions drop in from above

Script families

Six kinds of script, colour-coded throughout

PP1_SP

Locks round out1. Runs when the witness spends it. Rebuilds its own round byte for byte and certifies the next verifier slot, rebuilding that slot transaction whole. The create branch certifies the genesis slot Y_0 the same way. 16,924 B

PP2

Locks round out2. Runs in the witness. Forces every witness input to come from this round and the witness output to pay the owner. 1,366 B

PP3

Locks round out3 and holds the pool balance. Runs when the next round spends it, at input 3. Pins the verifier slot that round must also spend, requires it to spend the anchor of the slot its own successor pins, and requires its output 3 to run this same program: a forward covenant, so the balance can never move into a weaker script. No owner and no burn path. 49,195 B

V

Locks slot transaction out0, one satoshi. Runs as input 2 of the round it verifies. A header push, then the signer's key hash, then the body: the signer check, the round's publics against both headers, the balance step, every output rebuilt against hashOutputs, and the root proof's verifier. 413,201 B at test parameters, 1,782,796 B and 823,536 opcodes in production.

Deposit covenant

Locks the depositor's output. Runs as input 5 or later of the round it names. Names PP3_N's outpoint, so only round N+1 can take it in, and only by writing the receipt for its commitment and whole value at the same index. Otherwise it goes back to the depositor from a block height. 1,310 B

P2PKH and metadata

Change out0, the metadata OP_FALSE OP_RETURN at out4, receipts and then withdrawals from out5, the witness's single ModP2PKH output, and the one-satoshi anchor on each slot transaction's out1. No covenant on any of them; V fixes their bytes, and the anchor matters because of who must spend it.

The arrangement

Three scripts, none of them sufficient alone

PP1 is the locking script on round N+1's output 1. It does not execute when that round is mined; it executes later, when witness N+1 spends it. By then the round is confirmed and its withdrawal outputs are already spendable, so PP1 can never gate its own round's money. That is why its checks point one round ahead.

ScriptLocksExecutes whenDoes what
V_N Y_N out0 round N+1 spends it at in2 Requires a SIGHASH_ALL signature from the signer its slot names, verifies the proof, rebuilds the round's outputs against hashOutputs, checks the withdrawal list and the balance equation. This is the gate on money, and it sits in the same transaction as the payout.
PP3_N round N out3 round N+1 spends it at in3 Requires the round's input 2 to be the outpoint it named, so V cannot simply be left out; its input 4 to be the anchor of the slot the round's own PP3 pins; and its output 3 to run PP3's own program, so the balance cannot move into a weaker script. Knows the address, not the tenant.
PP1_N round N out1 witness N spends it at in1 Certifies that the outpoint PP3_N names is the output 0 of a slot transaction of the fixed shape, holding this pool's verifier with header N and signer N. Can inspect, but arrives too late for its own round.
deposit D out1 round N+1 spends it at in5+ Requires input 3 to be the PP3_N it names and the output at its own index to be its receipt. V then ties that receipt to a receipt slot, and the proof ties the slot to the transfer that mints the depositor's note.
anchor Y_N out1 round N spends it at in4
(issuance: in1)		 Nothing in its script; the point is where it is spent. Because the round that pins Y_N spends it, Y_N is mined before the pin exists, so a coordinator cannot pin a slot that never reaches the chain.

PP3 supplies the timing and PP1 supplies the sight. The ordering is safe because round N+2 spends witness N+1's output 0, so witness N+1 always exists first and the certificate is in place before the pin it describes is ever tested.

Proving

One mode: users prove their spends, the coordinator folds them

Every deployment runs a coordinator with a prover, and that is the only shape the pool has. Proof work happens in two places, and the split is not a choice: it follows from who holds which key. Everything in the map above assumes this mode, which is why Y_N always carries exactly one verifier.

At the edge

The wallet proves its own spend

A spend needs a nullifier, nf = H(nk, rho), and nk = H(sk, tagNk) comes from the spending key. Only the key holder can produce one, so the coordinator cannot prove on anyone's behalf. The wallet builds the proof and hands it over.

Produces
one spend proof per transfer
Anchors to
any of the header's 4 recent cmRoots, so a proof stays valid for about four rounds

one read, one submission, no handshake: the ring is what lets it miss a round close without being rejected

At the coordinator

A recursive prover folds the round into one proof

The coordinator aggregates every spend in the round into a single root proof and the new header. Y_N carries one verifier, V_N, which verifies that one proof when the next round spends it at input 2.

Y_N outputs
2: V at output 0 and the anchor at output 1, a shape PP1 rebuilds byte for byte
Measured
about 220 s for a 256-transfer round on one machine
Needs
the recursion stack and machine time, on the coordinator's own machines; wallets never fold

The coordinator is not trusted in either role. A round can only be mined if V accepted it, and the design's failure mode for a dishonest coordinator is that the pool stops, not that funds move. V is real on this page, so that holds for the rounds drawn here.

Why there is no second mode. Until 2026-09-21 the pool also had a direct-slot mode, in which the coordinator proved nothing and Y_N carried K spend-verifier outputs plus an append slot. It was dropped for four reasons, recorded in the design's 11.11: its K + 1 outputs could not satisfy the fixed slot shape that forces V to be output 0, so keeping it meant weakening that property or maintaining a second PP1; the prover it existed to do without is built and measured; its rounds were dominated by Y, about 1.7 MB at K = 2; and K slots would have made PP1 know K when walking the round's inputs. If a coordinator that genuinely cannot prove is ever needed, it comes back as a separate archetype with its own PP1, never as a mode of this one.

The slot transaction under attack

Six ways to compromise Y, and where each one stands

Y is built by the coordinator, certified by PP1 in the witness of the round that pins it, anchored by that round, and spent by the round after. Every outsider-triggered freeze is now closed; what remains can only be triggered by the coordinator, and it freezes rather than steals. The survey is recorded in the design's 11.15; this is its outcome.

VectorAttackWorst caseStands
1 Copy the round's proof from the mempool and spend Y_N out0 in some other transaction, which leaves PP3_N unspendable. Freeze closed by designV demands the named signer's SIGHASH_ALL signature, and a round signed by any other key is refused.
2 Malleate Y's txid in relay so that a different Y is mined. Delay delay onlyThe round spends the original Y's anchor, so it cannot be mined without that Y; rebuild and resubmit.
3 Double-spend Y's funding, get Y rejected by policy, or withhold the anchor key. Delay delay onlyThe anchor means the pin never exists without Y, so the round simply is not mined yet.
4 Pin a Y whose content PP1 will not certify. Freeze acceptedOnly the coordinator can do it, and the result is death, not theft. Closing it on chain costs about 3 MB a round, and the same coordinator can freeze the pool more cheaply by never spending witness N's output 0. checkSlotIsCertifiable refuses to build such a round.
5 Pin a decoy Y_0 at issuance that create never looked at. Theft closed 62b48ebCreate certifies Y_0 and the issuance spends its anchor. Holds while PP3_0 is canonical, which is the depositor's check.
6 Give Y a second input, extra outputs, a long scriptSig, or V somewhere other than output 0. Theft closedPP1 rebuilds Y whole from its parts and checks the input's length, so only the fixed shape hashes to the pinned txid.

Status

What is real, and what the chains simplify

  • V is real, at both scales. Its body is 412,942 bytes at test parameters and 1,782,537 bytes and 823,536 opcodes in production, under the 1M opcode limit. Removing any one of its checks lets that check's attack through. The node accepted the production round 1, which runs V, in 963 ms (914 ms and 957 ms for rounds 1 and 2 on the latest run), and its limit for a non-standard transaction is 1,000 ms. That margin is the thinnest number on this page.
  • The verifier body rides in every witness, by design. PP1 has to certify what sits at the outpoint PP3 pins, and the only link to that outpoint is its txid, which is a hash over every byte of Y. The body comes after Y's own input and the header, so no reusable midstate can skip it. The witness is where TSL1 pays for bytes once, so this is V's second and last copy. That is 72% of witness 1 at test parameters and 81% in production, 1.78 MB per witness against the 1.5 MB the design budgeted.
  • Deposits and withdrawals are gated. The deposit covenant, the depositor's payment and refund, and the coordinator's search are built. V gates every withdrawal in the round that pays it. A deposit is valid for one round.
  • Both chains are mined. test/pool_localnet_test.dart funds every transaction from real outputs, prices each fee from its size, and mines it before building the next, on the regtest node of ../localnet. The node needed maxscriptsizepolicy raised from its 500,000-byte default: every witness's PP1 unlock is larger, at both scales. The test chain goes in through ARC. The production chain goes in through the node's RPC, because ARC v1.5.10 cannot parse a scriptSig over 1,636,802 bytes and every production witness's is larger.
  • The node found what the interpreter could not. PP1's round branch left five items under its result. Consensus accepts that, but nodes relay only transactions whose scripts leave one item, and dartsv checks that only for version-1 transactions. Fixed in 8fde99c, and the tests now count the stack.
  • The notes are real, and a reader rebuilds both chains. Since the pool core change, each round's one real transfer carries hybrid-KEM bundles to real addresses and padding pays the public padding note with an empty bundle. ShieldedLedger rebuilds every header from the mined transactions alone, in 0.56 to 0.72 s a production round, and the wallet's scanner finds its deposit and its change. A round of 256 real transfers would add about 0.94 MB of bundles to its witness.
  • Everything is accepted by the node. The issuance, both rounds, all three witnesses, three Ys, the deposit and the payee spending the withdrawal, at both scales: V twice, the deposit covenant, every PP1, PP2, PP3 and anchor spend.
